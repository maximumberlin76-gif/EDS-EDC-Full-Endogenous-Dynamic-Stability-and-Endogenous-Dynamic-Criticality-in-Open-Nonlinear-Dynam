PART I — Introduction

---

1A — Problem Statement

Modern systems are not static.

They are open, nonlinear, and dissipative processes
that exist through continuous exchange of energy,
information, and constraints.

However, most models still describe them
in terms of equilibrium or static states.

This creates a fundamental mismatch between:

mathematical description
and
physical reality

---

1B — Core Hypothesis

Stability is not a property of a state.

Stability is a property of a dynamically maintained regime.

---

1C — Conceptual Shift

Classical view:

system → state → equilibrium

Proposed view:

system → process → regime → stability

---

1D — Motivation

Across domains we observe:

systems persist while maintaining balance

collapse occurs when extraction exceeds synthesis

stability requires continuous compensation of losses

---

1E — Scope

This framework applies to:

physical systems
biological systems
cognitive systems
socio-economic systems

---

1F — Limitation

This work:

does not replace fundamental physics

does not claim universal completeness

provides a unifying stability criterion

---

1G — Core Principle

A system remains viable only if it maintains
a positive structural balance over time.

---

1H — Minimal Rule

Do not predict outcomes.

Control system parameters.
