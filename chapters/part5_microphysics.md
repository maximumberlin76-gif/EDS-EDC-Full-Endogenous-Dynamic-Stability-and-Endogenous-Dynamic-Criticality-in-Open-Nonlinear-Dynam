# PART V — MICROPHYSICAL LEVEL

## Chapter 5A — Coherence and Interaction

### Definitions

ψ(x,t) — wave function  
|ψ|² — probability density  
φ — phase  

Wave representation:

ψ = A · exp(iφ)

---

### Definition 5A.1 (Coherent State)

A state is coherent if:

Δφ → minimum

### Consequences

- constructive interference  
- collective system behavior  
- minimal information loss  

---

### Definition 5A.2 (Decoherence)

If:

Δφ ↑

Then:

- interference breaks  
- system splits into independent states  
- coherence is lost  

---

## Chapter 5B — Micro-Equivalent of EDS

Mapping:

- C(t) ~ degree of phase correlation  
- P(t) ~ interaction with environment (noise, losses)  

### Condition

If:

C > P → coherence is maintained  
P ≥ C → decoherence occurs  

---

## Chapter 5C — Physical Interpretation

At the microscopic level:

- all systems are wave-like  
- stability = preservation of coherence  
- decay = loss of phase information  

### Examples

- laser → high coherence  
- superconductivity → collective phase  
- quantum systems → noise-sensitive  

---

## Chapter 5D — Boundary Condition

Important:

- coherence is a measurable physical quantity  
- phase alignment is observable  

Core condition:

C > P → coherence retention  

---

## Chapter 5E — Casimir Effect

### Definition

The Casimir effect arises from modification of vacuum modes between boundaries.

---

### Ideal Form

F/A = − (π² ħ c) / (240 a⁴)

Where:

- a — distance between plates  

---

### Interpretation

The force emerges due to:

- restricted modes inside  
- full spectrum outside  

---

### Key Insight

The effect is not caused by “force of medium” but by:

modification of allowed field modes.

---

## Chapter 5F — Medium Response

### General Form

Response = χ(ω, k)

Where:

- χ — response function  
- ω — frequency  
- k — wave number  

---

### Insight

A medium is not defined by a single constant,
but by its response across scales.

## Chapter 5G — Dissipation in Space

### Statement

There is no universal drag coefficient in space.

---

### Mechanisms

- rarefied gas drag  
- dynamical friction  
- radiation losses  
- plasma interactions  

---

### Conclusion

Dissipation exists, but:

- it is weak  
- it is mechanism-dependent

## Chapter 5H — General Response Principle

Any medium responds to perturbations:

χ = χ(ω, k)

---

### Cases

- simple systems → χ ≈ const  
- complex systems → χ varies  

---

### Result

System behavior depends on scale and regime.

---

## Chapter 5J — Coherence vs Inhomogeneity

If:

χ(x) varies strongly → Δφ ↑ → C ↓  

If:

χ(x) is aligned → Δφ ↓ → C ↑

## Chapter 5K — Atom as Quantum Resonant System

### Statement

Atoms have discrete energy levels:

Eₙ

---

### Transition Condition

ΔE = hν

---

### Resonance

Occurs when:

ν = (Eₙ − Eₘ)/h

---

### Clarification

Atom is not a mechanical resonator.

It is a quantum system with resonant transitions.
