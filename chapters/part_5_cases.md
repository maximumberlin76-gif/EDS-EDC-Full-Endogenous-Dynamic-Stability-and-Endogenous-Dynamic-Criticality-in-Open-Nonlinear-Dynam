# PART V — Case Studies

## 5A — Physical System: Laser

A laser is an open nonlinear dissipative system driven by external energy input.

Energy pumping creates population inversion, while losses occur through radiation, cavity dissipation, scattering, and non-radiative processes.

A laser does not operate as a static object.

It operates as a dynamically retained coherent regime sustained by continuous energy flow, structural constraints, and coherence-preserving feedback.

### EDS Mapping

S(t) — instantaneous intensity of synthesis of positive structural work through pump-induced population inversion, coherent emission formation, cavity-supported amplification, and structure-forming optical dynamics.

W_S(T) — accumulated positive structural work over an operational time interval T, associated with retained coherent emission and sustained optical organization.

P(t) — destabilizing pressure: perturbations, mode competition, thermal load, non-radiative processes, cavity instability, and external disturbance.

D(t) — irreversible structural losses: radiative losses, cavity losses, scattering, absorption, decoherence, and dissipative optical degradation.

Δ(t) — instantaneous structural balance:

Δ(t) = S(t) − P(t) − D(t)

C(t) — parameter of general endogenous structural coherence determining the level of coherent optical regime integrity and dynamic stability over time.

### Structural Existence

Formal laser regime existence is supported when:

Δ(t) = S(t) − P(t) − D(t) > 0

Operational meaning:

- pump-induced structural synthesis exceeds destabilizing pressure and irreversible losses;

- coherent optical organization remains formally accessible;

- the laser regime can continue as an organized emission structure.

### Dynamic Stability

Real dynamic stability of the laser regime requires:

C(t) > P(t)

Operational meaning:

- general endogenous coherence of the optical regime exceeds destabilizing pressure;

- coherent emission remains dynamically retained;

- perturbations do not exceed the coherence capacity of the system.

### Regimes

If:

S(t) < P(t) + D(t)

then:

Δ(t) < 0

Operational meaning:

no retained coherent emission regime can be sustained.

If:

S(t) ≈ P(t) + D(t)

then:

Δ(t) ≈ 0

Operational meaning:

threshold behavior appears.

If:

S(t) > P(t) + D(t)

then:

Δ(t) > 0

Operational meaning:

formal coherent emission becomes accessible.

But real dynamic stability still requires:

C(t) > P(t)

### Interpretation

Laser operation corresponds to a quasi-stationary coherent regime sustained by energy flow.

However, positive structural balance alone does not prove full dynamic stability.

A laser may emit while approaching instability through mode competition, thermal drift, or loss of coherence.

Final distinction:

Δ(t) describes formal coherent regime existence.

C(t) > P(t) defines real dynamic stability over time.
