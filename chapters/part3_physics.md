## 3C — Structure vs Dissipation

Not all energy contributes equally to stability.

The framework separates:

### Structural Synthesis — S(t)

Energy contributing to:

- organization formation;

- subsystem compatibility;

- regenerative continuity;

- retained coherent structural dynamics.

### Dissipation — D(t)

Energy irreversibly lost through:

- entropy production;

- turbulence;

- decoherence;

- friction;

- irreversible degradation.

### Destabilizing Load — P(t)

Energy or operational activity that:

- consumes structural continuity;

- increases fragmentation pressure;

- or fails to contribute to regenerative organization.

Operationally:

two systems may consume comparable energy while exhibiting radically different retained structural stability depending on how energy is transformed.

Within EDS/EDC:

## 3I — Structural Existence vs Dynamic Stability

Two different conditions must always be separated.

### Structural Existence

A system continues to exist structurally if:

Δ(t) = S(t) − P(t) − D(t) > 0

Operational meaning:

- regenerative synthesis remains sufficient to preserve organized structure.

### Dynamic Stability

A system remains dynamically stable if:

C(t) > P(t)

Operational meaning:

- retained internally agreed coherent subsystem dynamics remain stronger than destabilizing fragmentation pressure.

Thus:

- structural persistence alone does not guarantee dynamically retained stability;

- retained coherent subsystem continuity is required for long-term operational integrity.
