PART III — Physical Interpretation

---

3A — Energy Balance

Let:

E(t) — total system energy

For an open system:

dE/dt = J_in − J_out

where:

J_in — incoming energy flux
J_out — outgoing energy flux

---

3B — Mapping to Model Variables

We relate:

S(t) ≈ η · J_in(t)
D(t) ≈ J_out(t)

where:

η ∈ (0,1] — efficiency of conversion into structure

P(t):

represents energy usage that does not contribute to structural growth

---

3C — Structure vs Dissipation

Not all energy contributes to stability.

We distinguish:

- energy forming structure (S)
- energy dissipated (D)
- energy extracted without structural contribution (P)

---

3D — Entropy and Open Systems

According to thermodynamics:

dS_entropy/dt ≥ 0

However:

local order may increase if entropy is exported

---

3E — Local Coherence Principle

A subsystem remains stable if:

S(J) ≥ P(t) + ε,  ε > 0

Interpretation:

- incoming flux sustains structure
- dissipation is locally compensated
- global entropy may still increase

---

3F — Nonequilibrium Systems

Real systems operate far from equilibrium.

Examples:

- lasers
- living systems
- climate systems

Stability is maintained through continuous flow.

---

3G — Stability as Flow Condition

A system remains stable if:

energy flow compensates losses

Equivalent to:

S ≥ P + D

---

3H — Coherence and Efficiency

Coherent states:

- reduce dissipation
- increase effective efficiency
- stabilize system dynamics

---

3I — Limitation

Precise decomposition of S, P, D depends on system definition.

The framework provides:

- a structural criterion
- not a unique decomposition

---

Conclusion

Dynamic stability in physical systems emerges from:

- continuous energy flow
- controlled dissipation
- maintenance of structural coherence

The EDS condition is consistent with thermodynamic principles for open systems.
