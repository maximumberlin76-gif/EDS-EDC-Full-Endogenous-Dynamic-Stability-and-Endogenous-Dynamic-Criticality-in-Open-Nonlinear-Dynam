# PART V — Case Studies

---

## 5A — Physical System: Laser

A laser is an open dissipative system driven by external energy input.

Energy pump creates population inversion, while losses occur through radiation and cavity dissipation.

Mapping:

S(t) — pump-induced structure (coherent emission)  
D(t) — radiative and cavity losses  
P(t) — perturbations and non-radiative processes  

Regimes:

If S < D:

no coherent emission

If S ≈ D:

threshold behavior

If S > D:

stable coherent output

Interpretation:

Laser operation corresponds to a quasi-stationary coherent regime sustained by energy flow.

---

## 5B — Biological System

Living systems maintain structure through continuous energy exchange.

Mapping:

S(t) — biosynthesis and repair  
D(t) — metabolic losses  
P(t) — environmental stress  

Condition:

S ≥ P + D

Failure:

S < P + D

leads to degradation and loss of function.

Interpretation:

Life is sustained dynamic balance, not static structure.

---

## 5C — Human System

Human stability depends on recovery, stress, and energy loss.

Mapping:

S(t) — recovery, growth, meaning  
P(t) — stress and external demands  
D(t) — fatigue and dissipation  

Condition:

S ≥ P + D

Failure mode:

S < P + D

leads to burnout and instability.

Interpretation:

Productivity without recovery reduces long-term stability.

---

## 5D — Socio-Economic System

Socio-economic systems combine exchange and synthesis.

Mapping:

S(t) — structural growth (knowledge, infrastructure, human capital)  
P(t) — extraction (debt, rent, resource drain)  
D(t) — inefficiency (friction, corruption, waste)  

Condition:

S ≥ P + D

If violated:

structural degradation occurs over time.

Interpretation:

Short-term growth without structural surplus leads to delayed instability.

---

## 5E — Artificial Intelligence Systems

AI systems are energy-intensive computational structures.

Mapping:

S(t) — useful output and problem-solving capacity  
P(t) — system complexity and maintenance  
D(t) — computational inefficiency and energy waste  

Observation:

Scaling increases both S and D.

Condition:

S ≥ P + D

If not satisfied:

the system becomes inefficient and unstable at scale.

Interpretation:

AI is viable only when useful structural output exceeds load and dissipation.

---

## Conclusion

Across all domains, the same structural condition applies:

S ≥ P + D

This suggests a common principle governing growth, stability, and collapse in complex systems.
