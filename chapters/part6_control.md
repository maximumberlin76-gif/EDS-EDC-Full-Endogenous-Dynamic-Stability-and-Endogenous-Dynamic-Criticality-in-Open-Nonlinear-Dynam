PART VI — Control and Parameters

---

6A — Control Principle

The goal is not to predict system behavior,
but to control its governing parameters.

Stability is achieved by maintaining:

S(t) ≥ P(t) + D(t)

over time.

---

6B — Weighted Stability Variable

Define:

Δ(t) = α S(t) − β P(t) − γ D(t)

where:

α — synthesis efficiency
β — load sensitivity
γ — dissipation coefficient

---

6C — Stability Condition

System remains viable if:

mean_T[Δ(t)] > 0

over a given time horizon.

---

6D — Parameter Interpretation

α increases when:

- efficiency improves
- coherence is amplified
- structure formation is optimized

β increases when:

- external pressure grows
- extraction intensifies

γ increases when:

- losses rise
- inefficiencies accumulate

---

6E — Control Strategy

To maintain stability:

increase α

reduce β

minimize γ

---

6F — Feedback Structure

Define deviation:

e(t) = P(t) + D(t) − S(t)

Control objective:

e(t) → 0

---

6G — Adaptive Regulation

System adapts through:

- increasing synthesis capacity
- reducing load
- optimizing energy usage

---

6H — Failure Mode

If:

mean_T[Δ(t)] < 0

then:

- coherence decreases
- instability accumulates
- collapse becomes inevitable

---

6I — Interpretation

Control is not about eliminating fluctuations.

Control is about maintaining a positive balance
despite fluctuations.

---

Conclusion

Stable systems are not predicted.

They are actively maintained
through parameter control.
