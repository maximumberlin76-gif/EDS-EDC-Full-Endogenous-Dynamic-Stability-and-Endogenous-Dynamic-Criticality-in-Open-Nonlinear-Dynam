# Visualization Notes  
Interpretation of Kuramoto Synchronization Results

---

## 1. Purpose

This document explains how to interpret the visualization
of Kuramoto synchronization results in the context of EDS/EDC.

---

## 2. Key Variable

R(t) — global order parameter

R(t) serves as an operational proxy for coherence C(t).

---

## 3. Plot Structure

The plot contains:

- Baseline curve (no forcing)  
- Forced curve (external coherent forcing)  
- Threshold line (R_threshold ≈ 0.7)  

---

## 4. What to Look For

### 1. Convergence Speed

- Faster rise of R(t) → faster coherence formation  
- Indicates transition toward stable regime  

---

### 2. Maximum Coherence

- Higher R_max → stronger synchronization  
- Indicates higher structural coherence  

---

### 3. Threshold Crossing

- Crossing R_threshold → phase-locked regime  
- Staying above threshold → stability  

---

### 4. Stability of Curve

- Smooth plateau → stable regime  
- Fluctuations → noise-dominated behavior  

---

## 5. Baseline vs Forced

Baseline:

- slower convergence  
- lower R  
- higher sensitivity to noise  

Forced:

- faster convergence  
- higher R  
- more stable plateau  

---

## 6. Interpretation in EDS Terms

Observed behavior corresponds to:

C(t) increasing faster than P(t)

Thus:

C(t) > P(t)

---

## 7. Interpretation in EDC Terms

Near the boundary:

C ≈ P

system becomes sensitive to forcing.

External forcing shifts system into:

coherent synchronized regime

---

## 8. Visual Signature of Stability

Stable regime is identified by:

- R(t) → constant high value  
- minimal fluctuation  
- sustained plateau  

---

## 9. Visual Signature of Instability

Unstable regime shows:

- low R(t)  
- irregular fluctuations  
- no stable plateau  

---

## 10. Conclusion

The visualization provides direct evidence of:

- coherence emergence  
- transition dynamics  
- stability under forcing  

It connects simulation results with the EDS/EDC framework
through observable behavior of R(t).
