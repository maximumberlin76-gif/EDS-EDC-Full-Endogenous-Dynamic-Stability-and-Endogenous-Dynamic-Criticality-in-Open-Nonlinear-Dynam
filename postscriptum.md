Postscript: On Continuity of Regimes

A real system does not break at boundaries —
only our descriptions do.

In nonlinear, dynamic, dissipative systems,
there are no absolute opposites as separate realities.
There exists a continuous spectrum of regimes.

We observe:

- coherence growth (C > P)
- quasi-stationary balance (C ≈ P)
- decoherence and decay (C < P)

These are not different worlds,
but different modes of the same system.

What is often called a “singularity”
is not a physical limit of existence,
but a limit of the model that fails
to describe transitions between regimes.

Thus, no “end point” exists —
only unmodeled transitions.

Life (as accumulated coherence)
and decay (as loss of coherence)
are complementary expressions
of a unified dynamic process.

A complete theory must not terminate at divergence,
but must describe the continuity of transformation.
