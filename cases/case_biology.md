# Case: Biology  
Life as Dynamic Structural Stability

---

## 1. System

A living organism is an open nonlinear dissipative system.

It continuously exchanges matter and energy with the environment
while maintaining internal structure.

---

## 2. EDS Mapping

S(t) — synthesis (metabolism, growth, repair)  
P(t) — load (stress, resource demand, environmental pressure)  
D(t) — dissipation (entropy, degradation, inefficiency)  

C(t) — biological coherence (integrity, organization, function)

---

## 3. Dynamics

dC/dt = S − D  

Living systems must constantly rebuild themselves
to counteract dissipation.

---

## 4. Stability Condition

The organism remains viable if:

C(t) > P(t)

This means that its internal coherence
exceeds external and internal load.

---

## 5. Critical Boundary

When:

C(t) → P(t)

the organism enters a critical regime:

- loss of regulation  
- instability  
- onset of failure  

---

## 6. Failure Mode

If:

P(t) + D(t) > S(t)

then:

dC/dt < 0  

⇒ degradation accumulates  

⇒ system approaches collapse (death)

---

## 7. Why It Cannot Be Otherwise

A living system requires continuous structure maintenance.

If synthesis does not exceed load and dissipation:

structure degrades irreversibly.

Thus:

S > P + D  

⇒ equivalently:

C > P  

---

## 8. Nonlinear Effect

In biological systems:

small increases in synthesis may produce
disproportionately large increases in stability
due to nonlinear coupling and feedback.

---

## 9. Conclusion

Life is a direct realization of EDS:

an organism exists only while it maintains
positive structural balance.

Biological stability is dynamic, not static.
